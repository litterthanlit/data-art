What you are looking at

Nine rivers of light, one for each region of the world. Each river enters at the rim in 1850 and flows inward year by year. The dark centre is 2025. As the light passes each year it takes that year's real temperature: deep violet and red when the region was cool, amber and white-hot when it was hot.

Per region stretches each river over its own coolest-to-hottest range, so every region shows its own change and every river ends white-hot. Shared puts all nine on one range, so you can see how unequal the warming is: the Arctic burns while the ocean and the Antarctic stay dim.

2025 against the 1901–2000 average

Annual temperature departures from NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance, 1850–2025. Values for the 1800s, especially near the poles, rest on sparse observations and carry wide uncertainty. Rivers that meet near the centre can braid into each other; each point of light keeps its own region's colour.
